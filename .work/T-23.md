- Piece 1 finding, for whoever picks up T-14 (or reopens solar/geometry.ts): (a) sunriseSunset()/
  halfDayHours() use a geometric horizon (zenith 90 deg); NOAA's tabulated sunrise/sunset uses the
  standard zenith 90.833 deg (34' refraction + 16' solar-disk radius), a structural ~4-6.5 min gap
  at Leh's latitude that no amount of numerical precision will close. (b) declination() (Cooper
  1969, single harmonic) has its modelled equinox at day-of-year 81 (22 Mar), ~1.4 days after the
  true equinox, costing 0.67-0.87 deg of peak altitude near 21 Mar/Sep specifically; solstice dates
  are accurate to 0.01-0.02 deg. Both are cited with derivations in validation-noaa.test.ts's file
  header. The four sunrise, four sunset, and two equinox-altitude comparisons are left as
  `it.skip` (title carries the measured numbers) rather than failing assertions, so `npx vitest
  run` stays green while the numbers are not lost.
